# Drawing provenance: the sr71.us "Fig71" sheets and the M-21/D-21 drawing

Checked 2026-10-04. Subject: the nine images in `model/plans/raw/sr71us/` taken from
http://www.sr71.us/schematic.html (Leland R. Haynes, "SR-71 Blackbirds", footer "Copyright 1996
Leland R. Haynes"; tier D, private reference only).

## Bottom line

- **No U.S. government copy of any of the nine images was found.** Nothing was downloaded, so
  `model/plans/raw/gov/` was not created.
- **Fig71a to Fig71h are one drawing**, cut into eight tiles: a Lockheed-style configuration lines
  sheet comparing the "A-11" (the A-12 family), YF-12A, SR-71A, SR-71B and SR-71C, with numbered
  stations. Labels inside the drawing date it to about **1976 to 1982** (see "Dating evidence").
  It has no title block, drawing number, date or organisation in any tile, and the website never
  captioned or credited it.
- **M-21_Drawing_23_JULY_04.jpg** is a Lockheed engineering-style isometric of the M-21 carrying a
  D-21, with fuselage station and pylon station callouts. Best guess (low confidence): a figure from
  the Lockheed ADP M-21 loads report that Peter Merlin cites, G. J. Angerman, *Structural Criteria
  and Design Loads, M-21*, report SP-727, Lockheed Aircraft Corporation, Burbank, 1966. No public or
  government-held copy of SP-727 was found.
- Government funding does not, on its own, put a contractor drawing into a government archive.
  For these images the government-archive route came up empty. Until an original turns up, the
  honest citation is "Lockheed drawing (origin undocumented), as posted on sr71.us", and the images
  stay tier D.

## Dating evidence inside the Fig71 drawing

- "SR-71B/C" on the raised second canopy. The SR-71C first flew in 1969, so the drawing is 1969 or later.
- "SR-71A SPECIAL MISSION STINGER" carries the reference line to an extra station 21 well aft of the
  normal tail cone. This matches the extended "Big Tail" tail cone fitted to 64-17959 (first flown
  late 1975). It is the most likely meaning, but it is an inference, so the drawing is probably 1975 or later.
- The A-12 is called "A-11", the only name the public had for the family until the CIA allowed
  "A-12" in Kelly Johnson's 1981 Lockheed Horizons paper (CIA release CIA-RDP90B00170R000200230054-9
  is the clearance letter). A Lockheed drawing meant to be shown outside the programme would most
  likely have used "A-11" before about 1982.
- The stations are plain numbers 1 to 20 and WS-1 to WS-4, not inch fuselage stations. There is a
  scale bar, the lettering is by hand, and the notes about fin projection are there to help someone
  read the views. Together these suggest a reference drawing for illustrators or scale-model
  pattern makers, not a production drawing (which would use F.S. inches and carry a title block).
  This is an inference.
- Hypothesis, unverified: Jay Miller's Aerofax Minigraph 1 credits "reference drawings courtesy John
  Andrews/Testor Corporation" (page 11). The Testor kits covered the A-12, YF-12A and SR-71
  variants, so a Lockheed-supplied variant comparison sheet like this one would fit. The Minigraph
  does not reproduce the Fig71 drawing itself.

## Web trail (Wayback Machine, CDX checked)

- `wvi.com/~lelandh/schematic.html` captures from 2001-01-26 to 2002-02-09 ("Revised March 29,
  2001") have no Fig71 images. The capture of 2002-08-08 ("Revised May 02, 2002") has all eight.
  **So the images were added between February and May 2002**, most likely in the 2 May 2002 revision.
  The site moved to `wvi.com/~sr71webmaster/` in 2004 and to `sr71.us` later.
- The section heading on the page is "A11; YF-12A and SR-71 A&B Variations". There is no caption or
  credit. The page's credit line, "Credit to Dryden Flight Research Center (NASA) as indicated on
  the drawings", was already there in 2001, before Fig71 was added. It refers to the
  `schematic1-12.gif` images, which are NASA Dryden graphics (EG-0075-01 to -05, EG-0106-01; the
  Dryden gallery files were checked and are different drawings).
- The Wayback Machine never captured the Fig71 image files on their own. CDX shows no `Fig*`
  files at all under any of the three hosts.
- File properties: all eight are 8-bit grayscale JPEGs at 300 dpi with no EXIF data, which fits a
  flatbed scan of a printed page. At 300 dpi the plan view would be about 2.1 x 4.1 in, the size of
  a printed book or magazine figure. "Fig71" may therefore be a figure or page number in a printed
  source, or just the webmaster's own name for "SR-71 figures". Neither was confirmed.

## Rights as each source states it

- sr71.us page: "Copyright © 1996 Leland R. Haynes". The site also says reproduction is prohibited
  (see `sources.md`). The site states no rights for the drawings themselves, and Lockheed Martin is
  the likely owner of the underlying drawing.
- NTRS, for the related NASA items: API field `copyright.determinationType` reads
  `GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED` (20000027440, 19990110312, 20000012490) and `PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED`
  (20090007797, 20120013451).
- CIA: the Johnson-paper documents are stamped "Approved For Release 2008/07/24" (or 2011/09/27) with
  their CIA-RDP numbers.
- NARA: Johnson's *Studies in Intelligence* article (NAID 7283113) has use restriction "Unrestricted".

## Related government-held geometry (not the originals, possible substitutes)

- NASA CP-1999-209699, *High-Speed Research: 1994 Sonic Boom Workshop* (NTRS 20000027440,
  `GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED`). Pages 207 to 225 are Morgenstern, Bruns and Camacho (McDonnell Douglas),
  "SR-71A Reduced Sonic Boom Modification Design", with SR-71A area-distribution plots. Pages 227 to 240
  are Fouladi (Lockheed Engineering & Sciences), CFD with surface grids of the unmodified SR-71. Both are
  contractor work, so cite and link (tier C). They give area distributions, not section outlines.
- NTRS 19990110312, *Study on the Modifications Required to Re-Engine the Lockheed D-21 Drone* (1999),
  includes 1999 Lockheed Martin D-21 modification layout drawings with sections and F.S. callouts. It
  does not show the M-21 or the mated pair.
